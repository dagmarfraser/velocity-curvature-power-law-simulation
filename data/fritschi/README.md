# Fritschi, Seiler & Gross (2021) — Velocity-Based Training Device Validation

A validation study of wearable/mobile devices for measuring barbell velocity during resistance training, against 3D motion capture as ground truth.

**Citation:** Fritschi, R., Seiler, J., & Gross, M. (2021). Validity and effects of placement of velocity-based training devices. *Sports*, 9(9), 123.
**DOI:** [10.3390/sports9090123](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports9090123)

**Used in this project:** not used. Assessed as a candidate for cross-checking position vs velocity spectral characteristics and rejected — the "velocity" devices tested (GymAware, 1080 Quantum, laser-based Flex) all derive velocity by differentiating a position/displacement signal internally, so they don't provide an independent velocity measurement; individual movement epochs are also far too short for this project's spectral methods; and the underlying raw time-series data isn't publicly available (only per-repetition summary values are). Only the paper's own supplementary figures/tables are present in this folder. Kept here pending a decision on whether to remove it entirely.

## Redistribution

Supplementary materials are Open Access via MDPI (DOI above); not separately redistributed here.

## About the data

- Vicon 3D motion capture plus several wearable/mobile velocity-based-training devices (GymAware, 1080 Quantum, Vmaxpro, Push, Flex), mounted on a barbell.
- Ten participants performing squat, barbell row, and deadlift exercises.
