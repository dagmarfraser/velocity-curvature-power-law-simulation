# Hickman et al. — Parkinson's and Haloperidol Shape Tracing

Two studies of dopaminergic manipulation and shape-tracing kinematics: Parkinson's patients on/off medication (Study 1), and neurotypical adults on/off a haloperidol challenge (Study 2).

**Citation:** Hickman, L. J., Sowden-Carvalho, S. L., Fraser, D. S., Schuster, B. A., Rybicki, A. J., Galea, J. M., & Cook, J. L. (2024). Dopaminergic manipulations affect the modulation and meta-modulation of movement speed: Evidence from two pharmacological interventions. *Behavioural Brain Research*, 474, 115213.
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbr.2024.115213](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2024.115213)

Related: Hickman, L. J., Fraser, D. S., Galea, J. M., Happé, F., & Cook, J. L. (2026). An assessment of autistic and parkinsonian movement profiles to inform selective classification algorithms. *Journal of Neurodevelopmental Disorders*, 18, 8. DOI: [10.1186/s11689-025-09668-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s11689-025-09668-8)

**Used in this project:** core empirical validation dataset (Study 2, PLACEBO/HALOPERIDOL). Study 1 (Parkinson's ON/OFF) is not currently part of the primary analysis (its 60 Hz sampling rate is unreliable for one of this project's six analytical pipelines).

## Redistribution

Birmingham data, not redistributed through this repository. Will be shared via UBIRA eData in due course.

## About the data

- WACOM digitising tablet, 133 Hz (Study 2); Galaxy Tab A7, 60 Hz (Study 1).
- Study 2: neurotypical participants tracing template shapes under placebo vs a haloperidol (dopamine D2 antagonist) challenge, crossover design.
- Study 1: Parkinson's patients tracing template shapes on vs off their dopaminergic medication.
